Chillax · customer app · design note

The visit tab

One second tab for every cafe: the customer's place in the cafe right now, whether that is a PlayStation room with a clock, a table with a sticker, or nothing yet.

Branch: feature/places Written 17 Sep 2026 Scope: client_web first, client_app to follow

The problem it solves

The app had four tabs: Menu, Rooms, Orders, Profile. The Rooms tab is built for a cafe that rents rooms by the hour. A cafe that only has tables has nothing to put there, and hiding the tab leaves a three-tab app that looks like a cut-down product. At the same time a scanned table has no home of its own: the only place to call a waiter is a small chip in the top bar, hidden behind a popover, on four routes, on phones only.

Both problems are one problem. The second tab is about the wrong noun.

The booking list always exists, and the table is behind its chip. People at a table book rooms, so the second tab is the places to book whenever there are any; only a running clock takes it over. A scanned table shows as a chip in the bar on every tab, and tapping the chip slides the table up as a sheet: the hero, the waiter and the bill, the tab, the way out. In a branch with nothing to book the table is the tab itself. Bills keep their own tab. Nothing is configured.

The states

The tab reads two inputs. Has timed places is the branch's list of active timed places being non-empty, which the tab already fetches. Where the customer is comes from the stored scan, the pending hold, or the running stay, in that order of precedence reversed: a running stay always wins, then a hold, then a scanned table.

The label change is the feedback. Scanning a code and watching the tab turn into "Table 4" tells the customer it worked. No toast needed.

The screens

Sketches, not pixels. Arabic copy is listed under each one; the app renders right-to-left and every label already exists in the i18n table except the new ones marked.

Rules that keep it honest

  • Derived, never configured. "Has timed places" is the list being non-empty. No branch flag, no admin screen, no way for the two to disagree.
  • A running stay always wins. A table scanned earlier never covers a clock that started for the customer. Same precedence checkout already uses.
  • No manual table number. The scan is the only way in on purpose. Typing a number is a second way to fake presence and adds nothing for an honest customer.
  • The web explains the scan, the app performs it. A browser customer already scanned with the phone camera to get here; the card says so and offers install underneath, since a customer at a table is the best moment to install.
  • Every width. The table view and the menu card render on tablet and desktop too. The side rail on wide screens follows the same label rule as the bottom bar.
  • No timers. A sitting ends when something real ends it: the bill is paid or voided (every phone that scanned the table hears it over the hub, ordered or not), the clock on a timed table stops, the customer leaves the table, scans another, or the cafe's business day rolls over. A table carried into a new browser session is asked about ("Still at Table 4?") before anything is sent to it. No clock guesses when someone left.
  • The tab is for the people on it. Having the table's link is not being at the table, so the table's orders are only returned to a caller who has an unpaid order there themselves. A friend joins the view by ordering a drink; a stranger with the link sees an invitation to order and nothing else. Names on the tab are the names people gave at checkout; phones never leave the till.
  • Guests included. A guest at a table can call a waiter and see the feed. Only ordering keeps the existing guest gate.

Build order, web first

Each phase ships on its own. The first two need no server change.

1

The adaptive tab

client_web only · built

One hook returns the tab's state and label. The bottom bar and side rail use it. The places route renders today's list, and exists only where there is something to book. The list gets the dashed scan footer, hidden while a table is scanned. The table view starts with the waiter and bill actions moved out of the chip, as pills with the existing local cooldown.

2

The menu card

client_web only · dropped

Built, then removed: on the phone it sat directly under the top-bar chip that already names the table and holds the same two actions. The chip is the menu's table presence; the places tab is the table's home.

3

The request that answers back

Notification.API + client_web · built (the stay view's quick actions still use their own cooldown)

A read of the customer's open requests, a hub push on acknowledge and complete, and a cancel while pending. The pill moves through sent, seen, on the way, done, and replaces the timers. Staff side: "I'm going" is one tap with a sound.

4

The table feed

Ordering.API + client_web · built, then dropped

A running tab of everyone's rounds under the table and the clock, gated on having ordered there. Removed from both: a long list of items next to the waiter button reads as a bill and can put people off ordering more. The orders stay on the Bills tab. The Ordering endpoint for the place's open orders is still there, unused.

5

Keeping the tabs apart

client_web only · built

Four tabs stay: Menu, the visit, Bills, Profile. Two other shapes were tried and dropped — three tabs with receipts under Account, and an order-on-its-way line on the menu — because a customer with many orders would bloat the menu, and orders should never sit beside the places to book. What stayed from the pass: the rating on the paid bill, the place's tab under a running clock, and the branch switch confirming that it leaves the table.

5b

The orders tab as bills — renamed الحساب

Sales + Notification + client_web · built

The cafe sells time as well as drinks, and a customer can be on two unsettled tickets at once — the table they started at and the room they moved to. Orders alone could not say what was on them, so the tab reads bills instead: one Sales read returns every ticket the caller is on (sat in the room, ordered a line, paid a share), open ones whatever their age and closed ones for the last ninety days, with lines, the till's totals and the caller's own lines flagged. Guests are known by the same header Ordering uses. The tab is now "Bills" — "الحساب", what a customer asks for at the table — on the route /bills, and the profile tab reads "البروفايل" rather than "حسابي" so the two never sound alike, and the English label of the places tab is "Book", as the Arabic "احجز" already was; the house account's labels say "balance" and "on your tab" for the same reason. The tile keeps to the customer's own rounds and shows the place's time whole (an even split by the stay's roster was tried and dropped, with the field Sales grew for it: the till decides the split at settle, so any number the app shows can be wrong; the roster now comes from the customer's own stays, only to tell a shared bill from one that is all theirs), ending on "your rounds" when the bill is shared, and a tap opens the bill on the receipt page — the printed receipt once paid, the slip otherwise, guests included; a friend's rounds listed under yours would read as a table feed, which was cut from the clock card for putting people off ordering. Over the open ones, the tab balance from Accounts, since only the till knows the customer's part of a shared bill and it says so by putting it on account ("on you today" summed from the bills was tried and dropped for guessing); a clock still running adds its time so far from the stay's segments, rounded the way the tariff rounds, marked as about. Orders the till has not confirmed wait above, since they are on no bill yet. The separate thanks card and its stored state went; the paid tile carries the receipt number and the stars. Each member of a stay now also hears the clock stop on their own hub group, so the time line lands without waiting for the next read.

Not in this note

Presence proof for a leaked link, and any per-sitting object for tables. Both were discussed and parked. What was built against the leaked link instead: the till's confirm is an identity check (account or guest, the phone, and how many orders the device has had confirmed here before, with "nobody at the table" cancelling the order and turning the device away for the day); the server allows one unconfirmed guest order per device per place and one open request of a kind per person per place, with rate limits per device and per address on both endpoints; and a branch can require an account for table orders.
